You've Got Your TCF Canada Score — What's Next?

Many people pour all their effort into hitting a target TCF Canada score, then stall out because they're not sure what comes after. The score is just one piece of an Express Entry application — using it correctly is what actually turns it into an advantage for your file.

Hai Bui · DALF C2, 50/50 on Speaking · Oui Academy

Your raw score needs converting before it means anything to your application

The raw score on your TCF Canada results slip isn't the number IRCC uses directly — it needs to be converted to the NCLC scale (Canadian Language Benchmarks) for each skill (Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing). That's the first thing to sort out before calculating anything else for your application.

1
Convert each skill's score to the NCLC scale

IRCC assesses language points by individual skill, not by an overall total. Confirm the NCLC level for all four skills before estimating your CRS points.

2
Update or create your Express Entry profile with the new result

If you already have an Express Entry profile, your French result needs to be entered so the system recalculates your CRS score. If you don't have a profile yet, this is the time to prepare the remaining documents to file.

3
Check whether you qualify for extra bilingual points

Some profiles get additional CRS points if both English and French meet certain thresholds. This is easy to miss if you're focused on just one language.

4
Track the result's validity window through the whole waiting period

From updating your profile to receiving an Invitation to Apply (ITA) can take time. Keep the 2-year expiry of your result in mind throughout the wait.

A note CRS scoring and NCLC conversion can change over time, and every application has its own factors (age, education, work experience, etc.) that affect the total score. This article is general orientation — for figures specific to your case, confirm directly with IRCC's official calculator or a licensed immigration consultant.

If the result isn't what you hoped for, don't rush straight into a retest

Plenty of people get a lower score than expected and register for a retest immediately without pinpointing what actually needs fixing. See when you should retake TCF Canada to avoid getting the same result twice.
