Can You Self-Study for DELF/DALF, or Do You Need a Tutor?

Grammar and vocabulary, you can self-study. But two skills almost always plateau without help — here's how to tell which stage you're in and what to do about it.

Hai Bui · DALF C2, 50/50 on Speaking · Oui Academy

What self-study handles well

Not every skill needs a teacher. For grammar, vocabulary, and reading comprehension, self-study can genuinely work if you have discipline and decent materials.

Self-study works

  • Grammar — books + repeated practice
  • Vocabulary — Anki, reading news, films
  • Reading — drill past papers, self-check answers

Almost always needs help

  • Speaking — needs real-time correction
  • Writing — you can't see your own structural flaws
  • "Defending an opinion" (B1+) — needs a real counter-arguer

Why Speaking and Writing almost always plateau alone

Speaking is the one skill that needs immediate, real-time feedback — you can't hear your own intonation or sentence-structure mistakes while you're speaking. Self-studying Speaking alone typically plateaus at "understandable but unnatural," and stops improving no matter how much you drill after that point.

Writing is the same problem — grading your own essay is nearly impossible, because you can't see the logical or structural flaws an examiner would actually deduct points for. You can write grammatically correct French and still lose heavily on disorganized argumentation, and self-study won't show you that.

For the "defend your opinion" section of Speaking (B1 and up), you need a real person pushing back to train your response reflexes — practicing alone can't recreate that exam-like pressure.

So where's the line between self-study and a tutor?

  • A1–A2: self-study works well if you're disciplined — you may only need outside help for Speaking.
  • B1 and up, especially with a certification goal: get someone to grade your Writing and drill Speaking with you — these are the two sections that decide pass/fail the most, and also the two that self-study struggles hardest to fix.
There's no one-size-fits-all answer How much you can self-study versus need support depends on your own discipline, how much time you have, and how urgent your goal is (an immigration or study-abroad deadline, for instance). The right split is different for everyone.
